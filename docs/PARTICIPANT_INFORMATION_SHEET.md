# Participant Information Sheet

**Study:** DemandIQ: forecasting daily customer demand for small food businesses in Kigali
**Researcher:** Kelvin Tawe, African Leadership University, Kigali (k.tawe@alustudent.com)
**Supervisor:** Simeon, Ethics in Software Engineering
**Version:** 1.0, 29 October 2026

## What this study is

I am building DemandIQ for my capstone at African Leadership University. It is meant to
tell a small food business roughly how many customers to expect the next day, so the
kitchen cooks closer to what it will sell. The forecasting models were trained on public
data from restaurants in Japan, not on data from Rwanda. Two things I do not know yet:
whether the app is usable in a real Kigali business, and whether the way it handles photos
of paper logs is acceptable to the people who write those logs. That is what I need your
help to find out.

## Why you are being asked

You are being asked because you keep a written record of daily customers or sales, and
your business is small enough that a forecasting tool has to earn its place. Taking part
is voluntary. If you say no, nothing changes for you.

## What taking part involves

We would meet once, for about 45 minutes, at your business or wherever suits you. I would
show you the app and you would try it: you enter a few days of customer counts, look at
tomorrow's forecast, open the explanation of what moved it. If you are willing, you
photograph one page of your paper log and upload it. The app will not accept the photo
until you tick that you have permission to share the page and that it shows no customer's
name or phone number. You then check every row it reads and fix what it got wrong.
Afterwards I would ask a few questions: what was clear, what was not, and whether you are
happy with how the app handles your photo. Skip anything you would rather not answer.

## What I would collect

I would collect the customer or sales counts you choose to enter, the one photo of a paper
log if you upload it, and my notes of your answers. I would not record audio unless you
tick the box for it on the consent form. I would not collect customer names, phone numbers,
staff records, prices or bank details. If a page holds any of these, cover them before you
take the photo.

## Where the data goes and how long it stays

Photos are read on the server that runs DemandIQ, which sits in Rwanda. This copy of the
app has no outside vision service switched on, so no photo leaves the country. A photo is
deleted automatically 30 days after upload. Ask me sooner and I will delete the import it
belongs to.

Only I see the data, and my supervisor only if you agree to that. Each workspace is
separate: a seat in one business cannot open another business's records. I hold the data
under Rwanda's Law No. 058/2021, and Appendix B of the assessment records what I do
with it. The full record is at `docs/PROCESSING_RECORD.md`.

## Your rights

The same law lets you ask to see your data, have it corrected or deleted, object to how it
is used, or take a copy elsewhere. You can withdraw your consent at any time. Email me and
I will answer within 30 days. If you think I have handled your data badly, you can complain
to the National Cyber Security Authority.

## Risks and benefits

The realistic risk is a page of your log with handwriting you would rather not hand over,
and a forecast that is wrong on a busy day. You can stop whenever you like. I will delete
the photo when you ask and destroy my notes. You will not get a working product out of
this. You get to see the forecast for your own counts, and your comments go into a student
project. That is the whole of the benefit.

## Research ethics approval

My Research Ethics Committee (REC) application is pending. I will not take a single real
record or photo from you until it is approved, and I will show you the approval before we
start. If it is not approved, we do not meet.
